Defining and Quantifying Oxygen Delivery Potency of Blood Products

A scientific perspective on measuring functional oxygen delivery beyond hemoglobin concentration.

Authors: Rogers SC, Brummet M, Tobin KV, Safari Z, Anand A, Bennett D, Adao R, McAslan E, Jacobsen M, Parrish A, Joshi A, Alp E, Zheleznyakova E, Buehler PW, Palmer AF, Salvi T, Beyer G, Khan MA, Renaldo A, Conway A, Rowden T, McGhee W, McCauley S, Sen Gupta A, Bruckman MA, Pawlowski CL, Shea S, Neal MD, Spinella PC, Pan D, Doctor A
01 · The Scientific Problem

Hemoglobin concentration does not fully describe oxygen-delivering capacity.

Current transfusion practices rely heavily on hemoglobin (Hb) levels to guide dosing. However, this approach overlooks potentially important differences in the actual oxygen-delivering capacity of different blood products.

The physiological question therefore extends beyond how much hemoglobin is present:

How much oxygen can a blood product actually deliver from the lungs to tissue?
02 · Measurement Concept

L-TOF — Lung-to-Tissue O₂ Flux

To address this question, the researchers developed an in vitro metric called L-TOF (lung-to-tissue O₂ flux).

L-TOF is intended to quantify the oxygen delivery potency of different hemoglobin-containing formulations.

The study evaluated oxygen delivery characteristics across several types of blood products and hemoglobin formulations, including:

  • Fresh and stored whole blood
  • Stored red blood cell concentrates
  • Artificial whole-blood analogs containing hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers
03 · Key Finding

Storage can change functional oxygen delivery potency.

Using L-TOF, the study found that red blood cell concentrates stored for 42 days demonstrated substantially reduced oxygen delivery potency.

300 mL Fresh red blood cells used as the reference volume.
476 mL 42-day-stored product required for equivalent oxygen delivery.
158% Reported increase in volume required for equivalent delivery.

The finding illustrates an important distinction: the amount of hemoglobin administered does not necessarily correspond directly to the functional oxygen delivery achieved.

04 · Significance

A functional quality metric for transfusion medicine.

L-TOF enables a quantitative comparison of oxygen delivery potency between different blood products.

Such a measurement could provide an additional dimension for evaluating blood-product quality and potentially support more precise transfusion decisions.

Oxygen delivery potency provides a functional dimension that complements hemoglobin concentration.
05 · Experimental Measurement

HEMOX Analyzer

Measurements were performed using a HEMOX analyzer (TCS Scientific Corp).

The instrument was used to generate oxygen association and dissociation curves for the investigated blood products.

O₂ Association Curves (OAC)

Association measurements were performed at a higher pH of approximately 7.6, representing conditions associated with oxygen loading in the lungs.

O₂ Dissociation Curves (ODC)

Dissociation measurements were performed at a lower pH of approximately 6.8, representing more acidic conditions associated with oxygen unloading in tissues.

06 · Physiological Context

Oxygen transport is a dynamic physiological process.

Oxygen delivery is determined not only by the amount of hemoglobin present, but also by the ability of hemoglobin to load oxygen, transport it through the circulation and release it under tissue conditions.

This places oxygen affinity, oxygen unloading, microvascular transport and tissue oxygen availability within the same physiological framework.

07 · BHOC Perspective

From hemoglobin concentration to oxygen delivery.

The concept of oxygen delivery potency is directly relevant to the development and characterization of hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers.

For BHOC, this raises a broader scientific question: how should the performance of an oxygen carrier be quantified when the ultimate objective is not simply to increase circulating hemoglobin, but to improve functional oxygen availability at the tissue level?

Precision Oxygen Therapeutics

BHOC is being developed from the perspective that oxygen therapy should ultimately be evaluated by functional oxygen delivery and tissue oxygenation, rather than by hemoglobin concentration alone.
