Defining and Quantifying Oxygen Delivery Potency of Blood Products

Moving beyond hemoglobin concentration toward functional measurement of oxygen delivery.

Authors: Rogers SC, Brummet M, Tobin KV, Safari Z, Anand A, Bennett D, Adao R, McAslan E, Jacobsen M, Parrish A, Joshi A, Alp E, Zheleznyakova E, Buehler PW, Palmer AF, Salvi T, Beyer G, Khan MA, Renaldo A, Conway A, Rowden T, McGhee W, McCauley S, Sen Gupta A, Bruckman MA, Pawlowski CL, Shea S, Neal MD, Spinella PC, Pan D, Doctor A
The Scientific Problem

Hemoglobin concentration does not fully describe oxygen-delivering capacity.

Current transfusion practices rely heavily on hemoglobin (Hb) levels to guide dosing. However, this approach may overlook significant differences in the actual oxygen-delivering capacity of different blood products.

This creates an important physiological question:

How much oxygen can a given blood product actually deliver from the lungs to tissue?
Measurement

L-TOF — Lung-to-Tissue O₂ Flux

To address this problem, the researchers developed an in vitro metric called L-TOF (lung-to-tissue O₂ flux).

L-TOF is designed to quantify the oxygen delivery potency of different hemoglobin-containing formulations.

The study evaluated multiple types of blood products and hemoglobin formulations, including:

  • Fresh and stored whole blood
  • Stored red blood cell concentrates
  • Artificial whole-blood analogs containing hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers
Key Finding

Storage was associated with a substantial reduction in oxygen delivery potency.

Using L-TOF, the researchers found that red blood cell concentrates stored for 42 days demonstrated reduced functional oxygen delivery potency.

300 mL Fresh red blood cells used as the reference volume.
476 mL Volume of 42-day-stored product required for equivalent oxygen delivery.
158% Reported increase in volume required for equivalent delivery.

This observation illustrates why the amount of hemoglobin administered may not necessarily be equivalent to the functional oxygen delivery achieved.

Significance

A functional quality metric for oxygen delivery.

L-TOF provides a quantitative framework for comparing oxygen delivery potency between different blood products.

Such a measurement could support more precise transfusion strategies and potentially improve the characterization of oxygen-carrying products, particularly in settings where oxygen delivery is critically important.

Oxygen delivery potency may provide a more functional dimension of blood-product quality than hemoglobin concentration alone.
Experimental Measurement

HEMOX Analyzer

Measurements were performed using a HEMOX analyzer (TCS Scientific Corp).

The instrument was used to generate two types of oxygen-binding curves for the tested blood products.

O₂ Association Curves (OAC)

Association curves were measured at a higher pH of approximately 7.6, representing conditions associated with oxygen loading in the lungs.

O₂ Dissociation Curves (ODC)

Dissociation curves were measured at a lower pH of approximately 6.8, representing more acidic conditions associated with oxygen unloading in tissues.

BHOC Perspective

From hemoglobin concentration to oxygen delivery.

The concept of oxygen delivery potency is highly relevant to the development of hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers.

For BHOC, this perspective supports a broader scientific question: how should the performance of an oxygen carrier be quantified when the ultimate objective is not simply to increase circulating hemoglobin, but to improve functional oxygen availability at the tissue level?

This distinction is central to the emerging field of Precision Oxygen Therapeutics.
