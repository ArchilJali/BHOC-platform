From oxygen science to clinical milestones.

Key achievements

A veterinary product in clinical use

The report records more than 100,000 Oxyglobin units sold since launch, following US FDA approval in 1998 and European Commission approval in 1999. This documents adoption of an oxygen-carrying drug for canine anemia; unit sales are not a count of patients or successful treatments. [PDF p. 4]

A defined human indication in South Africa

Biopure reports Hemopure approval in April 2001 for acutely anemic adult surgical patients, to eliminate, reduce or delay allogeneic red blood cell transfusion. This was a jurisdiction-specific milestone, not US approval. [PDF p. 6]

A substantial development programme

The report describes 22 clinical trials, over 1,300 enrolled participants and more than 800 Hemopure recipients. It reports completion of Phase III programmes and preparation of a US application. Trial participation alone does not establish safety or benefit. [PDF p. 8]

Why this matters

The historical achievement was making oxygen delivery a manufactured therapeutic function, supported by clinical development, regulatory work and veterinary use. The report’s “Oxygen Bridge” concept framed temporary oxygen-carrying support around patient need.

People behind the programme

Carl W. Rausch, a Biopure co-founder, was Chairman and Chief Executive Officer in the 2001 report. His shareholder letter describes the scientific, manufacturing and regulatory programme. [PDF p. 3]

Read the achievement in context

The US Hemopure application was still being prepared. Manufacturing validation and further financing remained necessary; planned facilities and projected profitability were forward-looking statements, not completed achievements. [PDF p. 3]

BHOC perspective · Precision Oxygenation Therapeutics

For BHOC and Precision Oxygenation Therapeutics, this history helps frame a practical question: which oxygen-delivery function does a patient need, and when? Relevance to emergency care is a research and development question; this report does not establish an emergency-use indication.
