# Oxyglobin / HBOC-301 — Regulatory Approval, Clinical Efficacy, Oxygen Delivery & Scientific Evidence

Oxyglobin (hemoglobin glutamer-200 [bovine], developed as HBOC-301) provides a historical veterinary evidence base for hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers. This page keeps regulatory approval, clinical efficacy, comparative transfusion evidence and mechanistic oxygen-delivery studies distinct from future BHOC development.

## Key evidence

### FDA pivotal field trial

The original FDA field trial enrolled **64 client-owned anaemic dogs**: 30 randomized to Oxyglobin and 34 to untreated control. Treatment success meant **no requirement for additional oxygen-carrying support during the following 24 hours**; it was not a survival endpoint.

- Efficacy population: **20/21 (95%) Oxyglobin vs 9/28 (32%) control; p≤0.001**.
- Intent-to-treat: **22/30 (73%) Oxyglobin vs 10/34 (29%) control; p≤0.001**.
- Plasma haemoglobin and Physical Condition Scale changes remained statistically significant through 24 hours.

[Open FDA Oxyglobin evidence](Vet-FDA-registry.html)

### Prospective randomized Oxyglobin vs packed-RBC comparison

**Zambelli AB, Leisewitz AL. 2009.** *A prospective, randomized comparison of Oxyglobin (HB-200) and packed red blood cell transfusion for canine babesiosis.* Journal of Veterinary Emergency and Critical Care. **PMID 19691591. DOI 10.1111/j.1476-4431.2009.00386.x.**

Twelve dogs with naturally occurring *Babesia rossi* anaemia were randomized to Oxyglobin or packed red blood cell transfusion. The study reported similar overall improvements in blood-gas, acid-base and blood-pressure measures, while dogs receiving packed cells tended to normalize habitus and appetite more rapidly.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19691591/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1476-4431.2009.00386.x)

### Distinct Driessen full paper — do not replace PMID 28404246

**Driessen B, Jahr JS, Lurie F, Griffey SM, Gunther RA. 2001.** *Effects of haemoglobin-based oxygen carrier hemoglobin glutamer-200 (bovine) on intestinal perfusion and oxygenation in a canine hypovolaemia model.* British Journal of Anaesthesia. **PMID 11575346. DOI 10.1093/bja/86.5.683.**

This is a distinct British Journal of Anaesthesia full paper and should be retained separately from the related Veterinary Anaesthesia and Analgesia record **PMID 28404246** rather than used to replace it.
