How to interpret these numbers

Counts are records carrying indexed species or model labels, not counts of confirmed treatment studies. A record can appear in several groups; group counts must not be added to obtain the total. Labels may be incomplete.

Exotic / other animals combines ferret, rabbit and other-animal labels, counting each record once. It does not imply wildlife use. Other / In vitro includes in-vitro labels and 13 records with no species label.

Journal names are normalized for the publication filter: known abbreviations and full-name variants are merged, while non-journal or unclassified source labels appear under Other.

Inside the original FDA record

95% treatment success.
A defined FDA endpoint.

In the FDA efficacy population, treatment success was reported in 20 of 21 Oxyglobin-treated dogs (95%) versus 9 of 28 controls (32%). In the intent-to-treat population, success was 22 of 30 (73%) versus 10 of 34 (29%).

Read the FDA study summary, pp. 4 to 8 ↗

Endpoint window
24 hours

Success meant no additional oxygen-carrying support for 24 hours; it was not a survival endpoint. The FDA report also documents adverse reactions, including pulmonary oedema. Results apply to this trial and product, not to every species or a future formulation.

Documented real-world case · Working K-9 · catastrophic trauma · Oxyglobin · 22 Aug 2002

K-9 Saxon: Oxyglobin, survival and the legacy of the Saxon Award

Fresno Police K-9 Saxon survived catastrophic line-of-duty injuries after being shot at point-blank range. In a letter reproduced in Biopure's 2002 Annual Report, Fresno Police Chief Jerry Dyer described Oxyglobin as playing a pivotal role in Saxon's survival. Fresno Police later established the Saxon Award in his honor.

Wodan: critical veterinary care, oxygen-carrying support and recovery

Bellwether No. 49 documented the treatment of Wodan, a five-year-old Belgian Malinois police dog transferred to Penn Vet's VHUP after severe line-of-duty trauma. Oxyglobin was recorded as one component of a broader intensive-care and surgical strategy.
