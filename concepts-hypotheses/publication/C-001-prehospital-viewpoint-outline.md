# C-001 Pre-Publication Viewpoint Outline

Working concept: **Are we identifying the oxygen-delivery problem early enough?**
Author: Archil Jaliashvili
Status: Stage 2 — Evidence review started
Started: 11 Sep 2026

## Working thesis

Prehospital hemorrhage decisions are necessarily made under severe time constraints. Existing transfusion-selection criteria use rapidly available clinical and hemodynamic signals, but the therapeutic objective is ultimately preservation of oxygen delivery to tissues. The publishable question is whether patient selection for future oxygen-delivery interventions can be improved by prospectively combining clinical deterioration with validated bedside perfusion or oxygenation signals.

This is not an argument to delay hemorrhage control or donor-blood transfusion, and it is not evidence that BHOC improves outcomes. It is a patient-selection and endpoint-development hypothesis.

## Evidence anchor already identified

- Warren et al. 2026: retrospective evaluation of proposed prehospital blood-transfusion criteria in 25,473 EMS-transported trauma patients.
- Criteria evaluated: SBP <70 mmHg; SBP <90 mmHg with HR ≥110 bpm; EMS-witnessed traumatic arrest.
- Among patients meeting at least one criterion, 52% received blood within four hours.
- Highest reported positive predictive value among the listed criteria was 56% for SBP <90 mmHg plus HR ≥110 bpm.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/10903127.2026.2723329

## Evidence review questions

1. What do current prehospital transfusion protocols use to decide who receives blood, and how much of that decision is symptom/hemodynamic driven because laboratory information is unavailable or delayed?
2. Which bedside variables have evidence for detecting worsening perfusion or oxygen-delivery failure before profound hypotension or arrest?
3. Which variables are feasible in moving ambulances, helicopters, military/austere environments and low-resource settings?
4. Which markers reflect systemic oxygen transport and which reflect local tissue perfusion/oxygenation?
5. Which endpoints would make a prospective patient-selection study clinically meaningful rather than merely technically measurable?

## Candidate variable families to review

- Mechanism and estimated severity of hemorrhage.
- Systolic blood pressure, heart rate, shock index and trends rather than isolated values.
- Mental status and other clinical signs of deterioration.
- Lactate and base deficit where rapid testing is feasible.
- Capillary refill / peripheral perfusion variables where validated.
- Near-infrared spectroscopy or other tissue-oxygenation approaches where operationally feasible.
- Microcirculatory or tissue CO2 approaches only if supported by practical prehospital evidence.

No candidate belongs in a proposed protocol until evidence, feasibility and confounding are reviewed.

## Proposed manuscript structure

1. **The prehospital decision problem** — treatment decisions occur before complete laboratory characterization.
2. **What current selection criteria actually predict** — need for later transfusion is not identical to present tissue oxygen-delivery failure.
3. **Oxygen delivery is the physiological objective** — distinguish hemoglobin quantity, circulation and tissue-level delivery.
4. **The missing selection layer** — review candidate bedside perfusion/oxygenation signals.
5. **A prospective framework to test** — define population, variables, intervention window and endpoints before discussing a product role.
6. **BHOC relevance and boundary** — future product-specific research only; no replacement claim and no clinical recommendation.

## Endpoint framework to develop

Primary endpoint candidates should prioritize clinically meaningful outcomes rather than “product administered.” Candidates for review include early physiological recovery, organ injury, transfusion requirement, complications, survival and prespecified composite endpoints. The final endpoint set must be justified against current trauma/EMS evidence and developed with emergency and trauma specialists.

## Immediate next work

- Expand the primary literature set on prehospital transfusion selection and timing.
- Add evidence on the relationship between Hb/Hct and immediate tissue oxygen-delivery assessment in acute hemorrhage.
- Separate validated practical bedside markers from interesting but operationally unrealistic technologies.
- Build a claim/evidence table before drafting manuscript prose.
- Identify 2–3 realistic target-journal article formats only after the argument and reference base are mature.
