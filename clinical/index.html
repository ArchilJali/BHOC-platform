01 / Why this matters

The treatment clock starts before the hospital.

Severe hemorrhage creates a period in which circulating volume, oxygen-carrying capacity, perfusion and transport time interact. The prehospital question is therefore not only whether transfusion is clinically valuable, but how oxygen-carrying support can be delivered safely across very different EMS environments.

  • Distance and time to definitive care
  • Blood storage, inventory and wastage constraints
  • Compatibility and operational requirements
  • Ground EMS, air medical, military and rural differences
  • Need for evidence-linked endpoints beyond a single hemoglobin value
Research question

The unresolved gap

The growth of prehospital blood programs demonstrates how important early oxygen-carrying support can be. It also makes the infrastructure requirement visible. A second gap can remain even after blood is available: restoring circulating volume and hemoglobin does not automatically guarantee adequate microvascular flow, oxygen unloading or tissue-level oxygenation.

Where could Precision Oxygen Therapeutics complement donor blood when blood is unavailable, delayed or operationally difficult to deploy — or when restoration of blood volume and hemoglobin alone does not adequately restore tissue oxygen delivery?

04 / BHOC research relevance

Explain relevance without overclaiming.

A short evidence-disciplined section would connect the clinical gap with properties relevant to oxygen-delivery research, while explicitly avoiding a universal “blood replacement” claim.

  • Rapidly deployable oxygen-carrying support as a research objective
  • Compatibility and storage as operational design questions
  • Microcirculation and tissue oxygenation as functional endpoints
  • Potential complementarity with whole blood / RBC pathways
  • Product-specific safety and vascular physiology remain essential
