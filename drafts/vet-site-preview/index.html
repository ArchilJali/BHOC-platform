Hemoglobin & Other Oxygen-Carrying Pigments

Nature evolved more than one molecular strategy for oxygen transport. Hemoglobin dominates vertebrate oxygen transport, but important exceptions and alternative respiratory pigments exist.

Fe Hemoglobin Iron-containing heme protein. The central blood oxygen carrier across mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians and the overwhelming majority of fish.
Cu Hemocyanin Copper-based respiratory pigment used by many arthropods and mollusks. Oxygenated hemolymph can appear blue.
Fe Hemerythrin Non-heme iron oxygen-binding pigment found in several groups of marine invertebrates.
Fe Chlorocruorin Heme-based extracellular respiratory pigment found in some annelid worms.
Hb Erythrocruorin Very large extracellular hemoglobin complexes found in some annelids and other invertebrates.
ØHb Antarctic Icefish A striking vertebrate exception: adult Channichthyidae lack functional hemoglobin and rely on extraordinary cardiovascular adaptations in cold, oxygen-rich water.
Fe ↔ Mg
A deeper molecular connection: heme and chlorophyll

Chlorophyll is not an animal oxygen carrier, and hemoglobin is not structurally the same molecule. But the heme group of hemoglobin and chlorophyll are built on related tetrapyrrole-ring chemistry. Heme centers an iron atom (Fe); chlorophyll centers magnesium (Mg). Nature uses related molecular architecture for very different biological roles — oxygen transport on one side, light capture and photosynthetic energy conversion on the other.

Scientific boundary: shared oxygen biology does not mean one formulation is automatically suitable for every species. Species physiology, dosing, safety and regulatory evidence remain essential.
