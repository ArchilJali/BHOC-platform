Strategic Stockpiles, Modular Manufacturing and Oxygen Security

A contextual analysis of resilient medical supply, Strategic Sovereign Reserves and the potential role of Precision Oxygen Therapeutics when donor-dependent oxygen-delivery systems are constrained.

Archil Jaliashvili · 28 September 2026 · BHOC Therapeutics
Contextual policy evidence. WHO sources cited here do not evaluate, endorse or validate BHOC.

Why this matters

WHO preparedness priorities increasingly address strategic stockpiles of essential medical supplies, resilient emergency supply chains, access to therapeutics and geographically diversified local and regional manufacturing. This creates a broader question for oxygen-delivery medicine: how can health systems preserve life-supporting capacity when normal supply systems are disrupted?

Blood supply is a strategic dependency

Donor blood remains indispensable medicine. Its availability depends on donor participation, collection, testing, processing, storage, trained personnel, transport and functioning supply chains. WHO guidance recognizes that infectious-disease outbreaks and emergencies can threaten blood sufficiency and safety and can disrupt donors, critical materials and equipment.

Could sufficiently validated, shelf-stable, donor-independent oxygen therapeutics eventually complement conventional blood systems as part of strategic medical preparedness?
BHOC Perspective

Precision Oxygen Therapeutics based on Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carriers could offer a fundamentally different preparedness capability: shelf-stable strategic reserves combined, subject to validation and regulatory qualification, with scalable regional manufacturing capacity.

From stockpiles to sovereign capacity

Strategic preparedness should not be measured only by how much material is stored. The longer-term objective is the ability to store, produce, quality-control and deploy life-supporting oxygen-delivery capacity when and where it is needed.

Modular robotic manufacturing

BHOC envisions modular, robotic manufacturing designed for scalable regional deployment, with centralized digital oversight and integrated QA/QC. This proposed approach requires facility-specific engineering, process validation, regulatory qualification, cybersecurity, supply-chain qualification and implementation before deployment.

Oxygen Security

We use Oxygen Security as an emerging BHOC policy concept: the ability of a health system to maintain access to clinically appropriate oxygen-delivery capacity during normal operations, emergencies and major disruptions. Further scientific and policy work is required to determine where oxygen therapeutics may appropriately contribute.

Contextual relevance to BHOC

BHOC is being developed within Precision Oxygen Therapeutics. Long-term development objectives include shelf stability, donor-independent production, compatibility without red-cell blood-group matching by design and scalable manufacturing. These objectives require product-specific analytical, manufacturing, preclinical, clinical and regulatory validation. WHO has not designated BHOC as a medical countermeasure, strategic-stockpile product or pandemic-preparedness technology.
