External Policy Evidence · WHO · 2026

Strategic Stockpiles, Local Manufacturing and Oxygen-Delivery Resilience

Contextual policy evidence. These WHO sources do not evaluate, endorse or validate BHOC.

Why this matters

Global health preparedness increasingly addresses continuity of essential medical technologies when normal healthcare and supply systems are disrupted. WHO planning for 2026–2027 includes strategic stockpiles of essential medical supplies, stronger emergency supply chains, research and development of medical countermeasures, and access to therapeutics. WHO also emphasizes geographically diversified local and regional manufacturing as part of resilient health systems and global health security.

Blood supply is a strategic dependency

Donor blood remains indispensable medicine, but availability depends on donor participation, collection, testing, processing, storage, trained personnel, transport and functioning supply chains. WHO guidance recognizes that infectious-disease outbreaks and emergencies can threaten blood sufficiency and safety. WHO's 2023 emergency guidance also identifies donor shortages and disruption of critical materials and equipment as causes of reduced blood availability.

Strategic reserves need different characteristics

An emergency reserve becomes particularly valuable when a medical technology can be positioned before a crisis, stored for extended periods and deployed without dependence on a continuously functioning donor network.

Could sufficiently validated, shelf-stable, donor-independent oxygen therapeutics eventually complement conventional blood systems as part of strategic medical preparedness?

Local manufacturing changes the equation

WHO's emphasis on geographically diversified manufacturing adds another dimension. Resilience can combine strategic reserves + regional manufacturing + distributed supply + validated emergency deployment. For oxygen therapeutics, this remains a research, manufacturing and regulatory question, not an established capability.

Contextual relevance to BHOC

BHOC, Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier, is being developed within the broader concept of Precision Oxygen Therapeutics. Long-term development objectives include shelf stability, donor-independent production, compatibility without red-cell blood-group matching by design, and scalable manufacturing. These objectives require product-specific analytical, manufacturing, preclinical, clinical and regulatory validation.

WHO has not designated BHOC as a medical countermeasure, strategic-stockpile product or pandemic-preparedness technology. The relevance of these WHO policies is contextual: they identify health-system needs against which future oxygen therapeutics can be scientifically and operationally evaluated.

Oxygen Security

We use Oxygen Security here as an emerging BHOC policy concept: the ability of a health system to maintain access to clinically appropriate oxygen-delivery capacity during normal operations, emergencies and major disruptions. Further scientific and policy work is required to define the concept and determine where oxygen therapeutics may appropriately contribute.
