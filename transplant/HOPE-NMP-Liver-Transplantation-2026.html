HOPE and NMP in liver transplantation

Two recent studies show how oxygenated machine perfusion is being used for donor liver assessment, organ viability and higher-risk graft selection.

Transplantation evidence hub →International cohort ↗Registry study ↗

Machine perfusion is increasingly used not only to support an organ during transport, but also to assess viability and guide the use of higher-risk donor grafts.

StudyReported signalEvidence boundary
International cohort
954 NMP-treated grafts and 1,202 HOPE-treated grafts.		In the reported NMP cohort, death-censored graft survival remained above 96% for DBD grafts and above 94% for DCD grafts through three years. After risk adjustment, outcomes were similar between NMP and HOPE.Donor-risk profiles differed between groups. This was a real-world cohort, not a randomized comparison.
National Organ Perfusion Registry
545 NMP liver transplants across nine centres.		NMP was used for DCD grafts, steatotic livers, older donor organs and expected prolonged cold ischaemia. Reported 90-day death-censored graft failure was 1.7%.No randomized control group was reported. Results from specialist centres may not apply to every setting.
What is changing: oxygenated machine perfusion is becoming a clinical platform for donor selection, organ assessment and the use of marginal grafts.

Relevance to future oxygen-delivery research

These studies do not evaluate BHOC and do not demonstrate BHOC efficacy. They identify a relevant research environment in which oxygen availability, tissue-level injury, viability assessment and perfusate design can be measured directly.

For future Precision Oxygen Therapeutics research, the question is whether an additional oxygen-delivery function could be integrated into established transplantation workflows with measurable safety and viability endpoints.

The objective is not to replace transplantation or machine-perfusion systems. It is to understand whether additional oxygen-delivery capability can help make more donor organs usable.

Evidence boundary: this page summarizes external transplantation research. It is not a BHOC clinical study, a regulatory claim or a recommendation for treatment.
