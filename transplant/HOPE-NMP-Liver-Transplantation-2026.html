Transplantation · External evidence update

Machine perfusion and transplantation

September 2026 evidence update across liver, kidney and lung transplantation, with a focus on preservation, perfusion, graft assessment and ischemia-reperfusion context.

Transplantation evidence hub →

Machine perfusion is increasingly used not only to support an organ during transport, but also to assess viability and guide the use of higher-risk donor grafts.

International cohort
954 NMP-treated grafts and 1,202 HOPE-treated grafts.		In the reported NMP cohort, death-censored graft survival remained above 96% for DBD grafts and above 94% for DCD grafts through three years. After risk adjustment, outcomes were similar between NMP and HOPE.Donor-risk profiles differed between groups. This was a real-world cohort, not a randomized comparison.
National Organ Perfusion Registry
545 NMP liver transplants across nine centres.		NMP was used for DCD grafts, steatotic livers, older donor organs and expected prolonged cold ischaemia. Reported 90-day death-censored graft failure was 1.7%.No randomized control group was reported. Results from specialist centres may not apply to every setting.

Liver · HOPE vs static cold storage
Meta-analysis of 8 randomized trials, 984 adult recipients.		HOPE was associated with lower early allograft dysfunction (17.8% vs 33.5%), primary non-function (0.4% vs 3.6%), major complications and biliary complications. One-year graft survival was comparable; retransplantation was lower with HOPE.Strong evidence that oxygenated preservation can modify early graft outcomes and ischemia-reperfusion-related morbidity. This is not BHOC evidence and does not establish a role for BHOC in perfusion.
Kidney · high-KDPI hypothermic machine perfusion
Single-center retrospective cohort, 71 recipients of KDPI ≥85 kidneys.		Pre-specified HMP flow/resistance groups did not show statistically significant differences in 1-year renal function, graft survival or patient survival; delayed graft function was numerically lower in the higher-flow/lower-resistance group (43% vs 61%).Useful for the question of which machine-perfusion measurements are truly predictive. It supports studying functional endpoints rather than assuming a perfusion parameter is a surrogate for tissue benefit.
Lung · TA-NRP procurement
OPTN retrospective analysis of DCD lung transplantation.		One- and three-year survival were comparable between TA-NRP and standard rapid recovery. After adjustment, TA-NRP recipients had lower odds of ECMO use at 72 hours; the authors concluded that short- and mid-term outcomes support the safety of the procurement technique.Relevant to regional perfusion, preservation and oxygen-delivery physiology across organs. Observational registry evidence; not a study of BHOC or of an oxygen carrier.
What is changing: transplantation increasingly uses perfusion as a measurable platform for preservation, donor selection, organ assessment and reperfusion-risk management. The scientific opportunity for BHOC is to define testable oxygen-delivery questions inside these established workflows, not to infer efficacy from them.

Relevance to future oxygen-delivery research

These studies do not evaluate BHOC and do not demonstrate BHOC efficacy. They identify a relevant research environment in which oxygen availability, tissue-level injury, viability assessment and perfusate design can be measured directly.

For future Precision Oxygen Therapeutics research, the question is whether an additional oxygen-delivery function could be integrated into established transplantation workflows with measurable safety and viability endpoints.

The objective is not to replace transplantation or machine-perfusion systems. It is to understand whether additional oxygen-delivery capability can help make more donor organs usable.

Evidence boundary: this page summarizes external transplantation research. It is not a BHOC clinical study, a regulatory claim or a recommendation for treatment.
